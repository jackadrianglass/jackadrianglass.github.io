---
title: Retreating from the Internet
description: I quit YouTube. And pretty much every other social media. Here's why
date: 2026-07-16 00:00:00
tags: ["life", "internet"]
---

Two months ago, I quit watching YouTube. This was after a series of quitting other things on the internet. I quit Netflix, Facebook, LinkedIn, and Instagram. YouTube happened to be the last to go.

I quit because I felt terrible. I'd wake up, consume content, go to work, come home, consume content, then go to bed. I had this constant feeling of being numb with a slight negative connotation. It was like something's always wrong, and the only way to not feel that feeling was to consume media. Every time the video stopped, the feelings returned-louder, more negative.

Finally, a thought hit me: Was I conditioning myself to be a passive consumer in every part of my life by only watching and never creating? Is every opinion that I have just a regurgitation from some online creator? I took stock of what I was doing. When I was in a group, I noticed that there was a very quiet zone that always seemed to be adjacent to me. When I was at home, I was always looking for something to distract myself from my own thoughts. The evidence seems to support the thought.

That's why I'm retreating from the mainstream internet. I want to escape the attention economy, where everything is getting progressively louder trying to capture my attention. I want to be the customer again, not the product. Ideally, the internet becomes quieter, with more people sharing what they deeply care about.

Here's to me switching from passive consumption to active creation. I'm going to try to write some music, build software that helps me, spend lots of time with my friends, and, in the end, live my life!

What will you do?
